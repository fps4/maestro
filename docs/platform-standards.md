# Platform Standards — What the Platform Builds To, and What It and Its Applications Can Claim

**Status:** Draft v0.6 — for refinement
**Companions:** `conceptual-design.md` (v1.0) is authoritative for *what* and *why*; `technical-design.md` (v1.0) for the service set, build order, and substrate; the PS designs for their own internals; `uc1-nl-construction.md` (v0.2) is the domain counterpart to this document and holds the *client's* obligations where this one holds the *platform's*. Precedence runs in that order and this document is wrong where it conflicts.
**Scope:** Four sets, deliberately kept apart (§1). **§3** — the Tier 1 invariant catalogue, including **§3.7**, the AI invariants, and **§3.8**, the runtime invariants. **§4** — the build standards the composition plane emits against. **§5–§6** — the control model, and what a **generated application** can claim by inheritance. **§7–§9** — the frameworks, the **client clause set** (§7.4–§7.5), and the regulation binding the platform itself. **Not domain standards** (the pack, and UC1 §3). **Not the standards engine** (PS5). **Not the pack registry** (PS11).
**Why this one:** §5.2 gave Tier 1 **seven examples** and called it a tier (corrected in conceptual v0.9); walking the design turns up **thirty-two** invariants of the same kind, never collected and therefore never evaluable, which **P2** says makes them worthless — and **§3.7 adds six more that no document asserts at all**. Separately, four questions have no home anywhere: *"are you ISO 27001?"* from a buyer, *"are you a CRA manufacturer?"* from a regulator, *"what can my application claim, given that you built it?"* from an Owner facing their own auditor, and — the one this version adds, now arriving ahead of every Dutch contract — ***"do you train on our data, and where is it processed?"***
**The rule this document is built on:** **claim only what you evidence, and never claim more than you inherit.** §5.3.1's four arrangements were written about external authorities for Tier 3; they apply unchanged to the platform's claims about itself, and unchanged again to an application's claims about the platform.
**Note on numbering:** v0.2 inserted §5 and §6; later sections renumbered. **v0.5 added §3.7, §7.4 and §7.5 and renumbered nothing**, deliberately — the cross-reference breakage v0.4 spent a change-log entry fixing is not worth a tidier outline. Bare §-references are to the **conceptual design** unless the section exists in this document; where both do — §7.3 is the clearest case — the conceptual one is named explicitly.

## 1. Four sets, and why they are four

A single word — *standard* — is doing four jobs. Collapsing any two is how a marketing claim ends up in a conformance record.

| | Subject | Authority | Evaluated by | Failure blocks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 invariants** (§3) | Generated applications, **and the platform's own services under D39** | The platform. There is no external body for *"tenant isolation is topological"* | **PS5**, on a specification and on runtime artifacts | A build or a release |
| **Build standards** (§4) | What the composition plane emits | External bodies (OWASP, NIST, W3C, OpenSSF), adopted by the platform | **PS5** at Verification (§10.3) | A build or a release |
| **Application conformance claims** (§5–§6) | A **generated application**, in its client's audit | External frameworks, **satisfied by inheritance from the platform** | PS12, continuously | Nothing technical. It blocks the **client's** audit |
| **Platform conformance** (§7–§9) | The **platform as an organisation and as a producer** | Certification bodies, auditors, regulators | An external audit, fed by platform-produced evidence | A **sale**, or triggers a **regulator** |

**The third set is new in v0.2 and is the commercially sharpest of the four.** A client does not buy conformance for its supplier; it buys conformance for the thing it runs. An aannemer whose ISMS is certified needs the applications inside that ISMS to be evidenced, and today the design produces no artifact that says which controls the application satisfies, which the platform satisfies on its behalf, and which are still theirs.

### 1.1 What P7 buys, and it is the commercial argument

> **P7 — every application produces its own evidence.** Audit trail, decision lineage, and conformance records are generated by construction.

For a normal SaaS, certification is archaeology: reconstruct who approved what, prove access was controlled, evidence a patch latency. **Every one of those artifacts is this platform's ordinary output** — PS2 holds attributed decisions with the oversight level in force, PS13 holds SBOMs and rollback targets, PS14 holds response times against commitments, PS12 holds the conformance record, PS15 holds what an agent tried and was refused.

**Extended by §6, the argument gets considerably stronger.** Because applications are *generated* against a bounded archetype set (§8), with identity, isolation, audit, and capability enforcement in the never-generated layer (§9), **a generated application inherits controls it did not implement and could not have got wrong.** That is not a marketing claim; it is what the generation boundary is for. What has been missing is the artifact that states it.

### 1.2 The client's AI clause set is not a fifth set

*New in v0.5, and stated here because the instinct §2 records — every gap looks like it wants a field — has a structural twin: **every new demand looks like it wants a set.***

Dutch clients now send an AI clause set ahead of the contract: *do not train on our data · tell us where it is processed · name your sub-processors · show us the logs.* Walked against §1's table it decomposes cleanly and adds no fifth row. *Do not train* and *process it here* are **Tier 1 invariants** (§3.7), because they are properties of the running system for which the platform is the authority. *Which model, at which version, and show me the log* is an **application conformance claim** (§6), fed by evidence PS15 already produces. *Are you ISO 42001* is **platform conformance** (§7.2). And the clause sets themselves — the EU MCC-AI and its Dutch wrappers — are **crosswalk targets and questionnaires**, never controls (§7.4).

**What the walk does surface is that one group of the first set does not exist.** Six of the demands land in Tier 1, and the catalogue has isolation, record, determinism, capability, data protection, supply chain — and nothing whatsoever about what leaves the platform to a model provider. That is §3.7, and it is the only genuinely new obligation this version adds. **§7.5 is the walk itself**, and its finding is that every absent item crosses one boundary.

## 2. Tier 1 against the standard object — four problems, and none of them was schema

*v0.1 found four problems with holding a Tier 1 standard in the conceptual design's §5.5 object and proposed three new fields. **v0.3 withdrew all three.** The four problems were real; none of them turned out to be a gap in the object. The section is kept rather than deleted because the review is the useful part, and because the same instinct will recur.*

The tier under examination: *"**Tier 1 — Platform standards.** How things must be built, regardless of domain. Never overridable."*

| # | Problem, as found | v0.3 verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **a** | **Tier 1 already has conditionality and pretends not to.** D28 binds it to what the platform *builds and operates*, not what it merely *observes*, so below N3 a violation is a recorded finding rather than a block. Add BIO for a tenant doing public work and the conditionality is per tenant as well | **Real, and a prose defect rather than a schema one.** `applies_when` always expressed it — D28's condition is literally the predicate `onboarding_level >= n3`. What was wrong was the tier's own sentence: *never overridable* was being read as *unconditional*, and it means *never waivable*. **Closed by a §5.2 clarification in conceptual v0.9**, not by a field |
| **b** | **Tier 1 has no interpreting party and some of it needs one.** The platform is genuinely the authority for *"tenant isolation is topological"*; a certification body is the authority for *"our ISMS satisfies ISO 27001 A.8.24"* | **Dissolved by §5, and the framing was the error.** An ISO control is not a Tier 1 standard — it is a **crosswalk target**. The Tier 1 set is §3's `PLAT-*` catalogue, authored by the platform, for which `interpreting_party: platform` is both correct and honest. v0.1 was treating a framework control as a standard, which is exactly the conflation §1's four sets exist to prevent |
| **c** | **Tier 1 conflates two subjects.** *"Every binding calculation is deterministic"* is a property of a generated application; *"no platform service names its substrate"* (T22) is a property of our own code | **Real, and not a field.** `applies_when: "spec.class == platform_service"` does the work, and the `Subject` column in §3's tables is catalogue metadata. Under **D39** the second kind becomes evaluable anyway, because the platform's services carry descriptive specifications — a payoff of self-onboarding nobody had noticed |
| **d** | **Some entries are not standards.** A lint rule in CI is an enforcement mechanism, not an assertion PS5 evaluates | **Real, and the consequence is V4 rather than a field.** How *we* enforce a standard is our implementation detail; §3's `Enforced by` column is catalogue metadata. What the problem actually surfaced is that six entries name an enforcement that does not exist |

**The general lesson, and the reason to keep the retraction:** *a field that restates an existing predicate is a second way to say one thing; a catalogue's metadata is not the artifact's schema; and a control borrowed from a framework is a crosswalk target, not a standard.* All three are easy mistakes when a document's job is to find gaps, because **every gap looks like it wants a field.**

**What survived the review is upstream and elsewhere.** D43 added three fields to §5.5 — but they came from the *domain* walk in `uc1-nl-construction.md` §3.5, not from this section. Tier 1 needed none of them.

## 3. The Tier 1 catalogue, consolidated

**Forty-six invariants — thirty-two gathered, six new in §3.7, eight new in §3.8.** The thirty-two come from §5.2, the technical design's T-decisions, and six service designs; **§5.2 names seven**, and the other twenty-five are decisions of identical force living outside the standards layer, enforced today by whoever remembers them. **§3.7's six and §3.8's eight are different in kind and the distinction is deliberate**: they are not consolidation, because no document in the estate asserted them at all before the version that added them.

**Two groups in fourteen days is a pattern worth naming, and it is not scope creep.** §3.7 arrived because a client demand crossed a boundary the catalogue had no group for; §3.8 arrived because a *substrate* decision did the same. **A group appears when a new boundary appears**, and the two new boundaries — the model port and the invocation admission point — are both places where something untrusted becomes something bounded. Those are exactly the places §9 says failures are catastrophic rather than visible, which is a reason to expect more groups rather than to be surprised by them.

### 3.7 AI processing and the model boundary

*New in v0.5, and the one group in this catalogue that is **not** consolidation. §7.5 walks the client clause set against the design and every absent requirement lands here, in a group that did not exist.*

| ID | Assertion | Subject | Enforced by | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PLAT-AI-001` | No tenant payload reaches a model provider except under a declared **egress class** whose data-use terms — no training, no secondary use, bounded retention — are recorded and in force at the time of the call | platform_service | runtime (PS15 mediator), PS7 | **V20** |
| `PLAT-AI-002` | Provider, model, version **and processing jurisdiction** are recorded on every proposed action | platform_service | runtime (PS15) | E12, **V21** |
| `PLAT-AI-003` | Minimisation before egress is a **refusal**, never a warning | platform_service | runtime (PS15 mediator) | V5, **V20** |
| `PLAT-AI-004` | An erasure receipt names provider-side retention it could not reach, and never reports complete for a copy it cannot see | both | runtime (PS7) | **V22**, PS7 §5 |
| `PLAT-AI-005` | A call that would process outside the tenant's bound jurisdiction is refused | both | runtime (PS15), tenant binding | **V21** |
| `PLAT-AI-006` | Any surface on which a human interacts with a model discloses it, and generated content is marked machine-readably | generated_application | PS5, composition plane | AI Act art 50, V7 |

**Two properties this group has and no other group in §3 does.**

**Every entry is enforced at one boundary** — PS15's model port and the mediator in front of it. That makes it the cheapest group in the catalogue to build and, like PS7 §4.2's classification envelope, close to impossible to retrofit: a payload that has already left under no recorded term cannot be given one afterwards.

**Five of the six are contractual before they are technical.** `PLAT-AI-001`'s terms are *procured*, not implemented — the mediator can only enforce a term that someone has obtained. That is **V-J**, and it is the only invariant in this document the platform cannot satisfy by writing code.

**Two observations that change what gets built when.**

**Under D39 this catalogue's first subject is the platform itself.** No interpreting party is needed because the platform is the authority for Tier 1, which makes a real slice of conformance evaluation first-wave against subjects we control — a considerably safer place to find PS5's defects than a client's estate.

**Six entries are `enforced_by: ci` and will be enforced by nobody.** T21, T22, T28's no-query-names-a-tenant rule, T30, E2's single path, and E3's empty tool directory describe their own enforcement as a lint rule or a code search, with no pipeline and no owner (**V4**).

### 3.8 Runtime and execution

*New in v0.6, and like §3.7 it is a new group rather than consolidation. It exists because T44 makes execution serverless, and the substrate decision converts four invariants that were previously matters of discipline into properties something can enforce — which under P2 is the difference between a standard and guidance.*

| ID | Assertion | Subject | Enforced by | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PLAT-RUN-001` | A runtime process is shared across applications **within** a tenant and never across tenants | platform_service | runtime (PS16 admission), deployment topology | **T45**, T6 |
| `PLAT-RUN-002` | A function has no general network egress; every outbound call routes through PS9 | both | network policy, PS9 | **T44**, D41, P8 |
| `PLAT-RUN-003` | An execution retains nothing between invocations; all application state is in PS7 | generated_application | runtime (PS16), design review | **T44**, T19 |
| `PLAT-RUN-004` | Credentials reaching an execution are invocation-scoped and expire with it; no long-lived credential is readable by application code | both | runtime (PS16), PS10 | **T44**, PS10 |
| `PLAT-RUN-005` | The tenant-scoped handle is bound from the instance record, never from anything the payload or the function supplies | both | runtime (PS16 admission) | **T44**, T28, T6 |
| `PLAT-RUN-006` | Every invocation carries an idempotency key derived from its trigger; effects at the PS7 and AE3 boundaries are keyed by it | both | runtime (PS16 admission) | **T50**, P3 |
| `PLAT-RUN-007` | An instance declares `hosted_by` and `operated_by` separately, and the platform performs no act on an instance whose declared authority does not carry it | both | runtime (PS16), PS5 | **T47**, P14, D29 |
| `PLAT-RUN-008` | A running instance's artifact digest matches PS13's record, continuously; a mismatch is a hard stop | both | runtime (PS16 reconciliation) | **T44**, X11, T10 |

**Three properties this group has, and the first is the interesting one.**

**Four of the eight are enforced by the substrate rather than by a check.** `PLAT-RUN-002` is a network policy, `PLAT-RUN-003` is the absence of anywhere to write, `PLAT-RUN-004` is a token lifetime, `PLAT-RUN-005` is the shape of the admission call. This is the opposite of **V4's** problem: those six invariants are enforced by a CI that does not exist, and these four are enforced by a substrate that cannot do otherwise. **An invariant whose violation is unrepresentable is the strongest form available**, and it is the argument for T44 restated in the catalogue's own vocabulary.

**`PLAT-RUN-006` is the one that would not have been written without the substrate change**, and it is the most likely to be lost if the substrate changes back. At-least-once delivery makes a deterministic calculation produce a duplicated *effect*: `PLAT-DET-001` holds and the number in the regulated record is still wrong. A long-running process hides this behind in-process deduplication that no standard describes, which is why it has been absent from a catalogue that has had a determinism group since v0.1.

**`PLAT-RUN-007` is D29 promoted from a contractual rule to a Tier 1 invariant.** T38 already made it mechanical at PS14's claim; this makes it mechanical at the act, which is the entry path a claim does not cover — an alert, an operator, or a remediation agent can reach a runtime without passing a work item.

## 4. Build standards — what the composition plane emits against

Tier 1 says what must be true. This section says what *good* means for the artifacts, and every entry is chosen because it is mechanically checkable at §10.3's **Verification** stage — which today lists *"conformance suite, golden cases, isolation tests, capability audit"* and nothing about the code.

### 4.1 Proportionality is the organising rule

**P10 applies here.** The assurance level of a generated application is a function of the consequence class assigned at Explore (§10.2) — the same mechanism that already sets governance weight and the oversight ceiling.

| Consequence class | Application security | Supply chain | Accessibility | Test bar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | ASVS L1 | SBOM, signed | WCAG 2.2 A | Golden cases |
| Medium | ASVS L1 + threat model | SBOM, signed, provenance | WCAG 2.2 AA | + isolation, capability audit |
| **High / binding / filing** | **ASVS L2** | **+ hermetic build, provenance verified** | **WCAG 2.2 AA + EN 301 549** | **+ determinism proof on every binding calculation** |

This is a better use of the consequence class than the design currently makes, which is to set governance weight and stop. §5.5 turns it into the industry's own machine-readable form.

### 4.2 The adopted set

| Area | Standard | Why this one | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Secure development** | **NIST SSDF (SP 800-218)**, with **800-218A** for the generative parts | The vocabulary every large buyer's questionnaire and every EU regulator now uses. 800-218A exists precisely for code produced with model assistance, which is what the composition plane does | Composition plane, CI |
| **Application security** | **OWASP ASVS**, level by consequence class | Verifiable requirement by requirement rather than a posture; maps onto §10.3's Verification stage as a checklist with evidence | PS5 at Verification |
| **AI application security** | **OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications**, with NIST AI RMF's *Measure* function | Prompt injection, insecure output handling, and model supply chain are the threat classes every client questionnaire names, and §4.2 had nothing for them. **PS15's posture is already the mitigation** — a tool catalogue that is a projection rather than an authoring surface, a model-facing process holding no credential and no egress but the model port, and refuse-at-first-failure — what is missing is the standard that says so | PS15, PS5 |
| **Supply chain** | **SLSA build levels**, **SBOM in CycloneDX or SPDX**, in-toto / Sigstore signing | T10 commits to SBOM and §10.3 to signatures; SLSA turns two artifacts into a claim | PS13 |
| **API and interface** | **OpenAPI**, **OAuth 2.1 / OIDC**, **MCP** | T17 puts UI, API and MCP on every service; naming the wire formats makes the triad checkable | Composition plane |
| **Accessibility** | **WCAG 2.2** at §4.1's level, **EN 301 549** where a public body is a user | §1.1 commits that a non-technical user never sees a console — the generated UI is the *only* surface | Composition plane, PS5 |
| **Observability** | **OpenTelemetry** | PS6 adopts a Grafana-class stack (T8); OTel keeps the generated dashboard portable at exit | PS6, generated apps |
| **Identifiers and time** | **UUIDv7**, **ISO 8601 / RFC 3339**, UTC | PS2 already specifies UUIDv7; stating it once stops each service choosing again | All |
| **Software quality vocabulary** | **ISO/IEC 25010** | Free, and it gives the non-functional half of a specification a vocabulary instead of adjectives | PS3 facets |
| **Architecture description** | **ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010**, notated as **C4** | §5.4 — and for a generated application it is **generated, not authored** | Composition plane |
| **Licence compliance** | **SPDX** identifiers, a declared licence policy | §13.5 hands repositories to the client; an unresolved copyleft dependency is an exit promise that fails on contact with their counsel | PS13, CI |

### 4.3 What is deliberately not adopted

Each will be proposed and each would cost more than it returns: a specific cloud CIS benchmark (substrate-naming, against T22); a language-specific style standard (generated code has no human reader, by P1); **ITIL** (§13.1's S1–S5 and PS14 already discharge the obligations, and a second vocabulary would put two names on every concept); and any maturity model — SAMM, C2M2, CMMI — which measures a *practice* rather than an *artifact* and therefore cannot be evaluated by PS5. **COBIT is the one exception in that last group and it is adopted for a different purpose entirely** (§7.3): not as controls to implement, but as the vocabulary that makes the governance model legible to an auditor who has never heard of this platform.

## 5. The control model — normalise once, crosswalk many

*New in v0.2. This is the section that makes §6 buildable rather than aspirational.*

### 5.1 The failure mode, stated first

The obvious approach is to map the platform onto ISO 27001, then onto NIST CSF, then onto SOC 2, then onto CCM. **Four mappings, each hand-maintained, each drifting independently, and every new framework multiplying the maintenance** — which is §15.3's standards-maintenance risk arriving in a second place, self-inflicted.

**The discipline the GRC industry settled on is one normalised control set plus crosswalks**, and the platform is unusually well placed to use it because **it already has the normalised set: §3's Tier 1 catalogue.** It was derived from the platform's own architecture rather than from any framework, which is exactly the property a normalised set needs.

### 5.2 One set, many crosswalks

```
                  ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐
   ISO 27001 ────▶│                                      │
   NIST CSF 2.0 ─▶│   §3 Tier 1 catalogue                │◀── evidence from
   SOC 2 TSC ────▶│   (the normalised control set)       │    PS1,2,7,12,13,14,15
   CSA CCM ──────▶│                                      │
   BIO ──────────▶│   + the build standards of §4        │
   800-53 ───────▶│                                      │
   ISO 42001 ────▶│                                      │
   EU MCC-AI ────▶└──────────────────────────────────────┘
        crosswalks                    │
     (pack data, not code)            ▼
                          per-application control matrix (§6.2)
```

**A crosswalk is a data file, not code**, and adding a framework is adding a file. That is the whole answer to *"flexible to expand in the future"*: the cost of the seventh framework is the same as the cost of the second, and none of them touch PS5.

**Where crosswalks live** is the platform pack, not a domain pack — a mapping from Tier 1 to ISO 27001 is not Dutch construction knowledge. This is UC1's **C-C** again (whether a platform pack is a pack), reached from a third direction.

### 5.3 OSCAL is the format, and it is the highest-leverage adoption in this document

**NIST OSCAL** (Open Security Controls Assessment Language) is the machine-readable format for exactly this problem, and its models map onto artifacts the platform already produces:

| OSCAL model | What it is | What produces it here |
|---|---|---|
| **Catalog** | A control set | §3's Tier 1 catalogue, plus each adopted framework |
| **Profile** | A tailored selection from a catalogue | **Consequence class** (§5.5) |
| **Component definition** | *"Here is what this component implements, and what is left to you"* | **One per platform service.** This *is* the inheritance statement (§6) |
| **System security plan** | Control implementation for one system | **Generated per application**, from its specification |
| **Assessment results** | What was checked and what was found | **PS5 output, via PS12** |
| **POA&M** | Open findings with owners and dates | **PS12's findings + PS14's remediation items** — the split W-D already draws |

**Four things follow, and none of them is speculative.**

- **The evidence artifacts already exist**; OSCAL is a serialisation of them, not new work. §10's evidence map is an SSP in prose.
- **A component definition per platform service is the single most valuable thing to write early.** It is small, it is stable, it is the input to every application's claim, and it is the artifact a client's auditor can consume directly.
- **It makes the crosswalk problem mechanical.** OSCAL's mapping constructs let a Tier 1 control point at ISO, CSF, and CCM entries simultaneously, maintained in one place.
- **It is open, it is a NIST format, and it hands over at exit** — which satisfies T21's spirit for the compliance layer rather than only for the runtime.

**The practical caution:** OSCAL is verbose and the tooling ecosystem is thin. Adopt it as the **export format**, not as the internal representation — PS5 and PS12 keep their own model and emit OSCAL, exactly as T19 keeps PS7's contract independent of any engine. Anything else makes a young NIST schema a dependency of the standards engine.

### 5.4 Architecture description: 42010 is the standard, C4 is the notation, and it is generated

**C4 is not a standard in this catalogue's sense** — no standards body, no versioned specification, no conformance criteria, and nothing PS5 can check. **ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010** is the citable instrument (stakeholders, concerns, viewpoints, views, correspondence rules), and C4 is one notation under it. That distinction matters the moment ISO 27001 A.8.27 (secure system architecture principles) or CRA Annex I asks us to evidence an architecture discipline: we cite 42010 and show C4.

**For a generated application the description is generated, not authored** — D38's precedent exactly, where the metric definitions and dashboard are generated and the runtime is provided. The viewpoints are fixed by the archetype set, the containers are the engines the specification wires, the external systems are the declared connectors. Nobody draws it and it cannot go stale.

**For the platform's own services, `c4-diagrams.md` is a hand-maintained descriptive specification in a second store**, which is the W-D shape arriving in documentation: under D39 those services already carry descriptive specifications in PS3, and the diagrams describe the same subject with no `verified_at`, no interval, and no PS12 to expire them. **It is not hypothetical — that document already cites technical design v0.6 against a current v0.7, maps containers to PS1–PS13 when PS14 and PS15 exist, and names three companion files that do not resolve.** Two days old. §14.4's *"a descriptive specification starts rotting the moment it is written"*, demonstrated on our own estate at no client's expense, which is what T27 says self-onboarding is for.

### 5.5 Consequence class selects a profile

§4.1's table is an OSCAL **profile** selection in everything but format. Making it one has three payoffs: P10's proportionality becomes machine-readable rather than a table in a document; a client's auditor can diff the profile applied to their application against the framework baseline; and adding a framework means adding three profiles (low, medium, high), not rewriting §4.1.

The natural baselines to align to are **NIST 800-53B's low / moderate / high** where a US or public-sector buyer is in play, and a locally defined triple otherwise. **The mapping from consequence class to profile is platform policy, not pack content** (V10, and the same argument as **C8** in UC1).

## 6. What a generated application can claim

*New in v0.2, and the answer to the Owner's question in the preamble.*

### 6.1 Three responsibility states, and the second one is where the work is

Every control, for every application, is in exactly one state. This is the shared-responsibility model every cloud provider ships, applied to a platform that has a much stronger claim to it than they do — because the application was *generated*, not merely *hosted*.

| State | Means | Application's contribution | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inherited** | The platform implements it entirely, below the generated layer | **None, and none is possible** | Tenant isolation, audit trail and attribution, secret custody, capability ceilings, artifact signing, rollback |
| **Shared** | The platform provides the mechanism; the **specification** must use it correctly | **A facet, a declaration, or a configuration choice** | Retention: PS7 enforces the rule, the specification declares the classification. Access: PS1 enforces roles, the specification declares which |
| **Client** | Outside the platform entirely | Everything | Staff screening, physical security, the decision to run the application at all |

**The shared row is the honest one and it is where claims actually break.** A control satisfied by the specification declaring something correctly is only as strong as the declaration — and facets are extracted by an agent and confirmed by a human (S4), with facet–body drift already named as a failure mode in PS3 §11. **So the strength of an application's compliance claim rests on facet quality**, which is a real and stateable limit rather than a hidden one.

**Inherited controls are the differentiator and they are unusually numerous here.** §9's never-generated row exists precisely because those are the layers where generated code fails catastrophically rather than visibly. Every entry in it is a control the application cannot get wrong, which is a claim a hand-built application in the same client cannot make about itself.

### 6.2 The control responsibility matrix, generated per application

The deliverable. One per application, produced from the specification, versioned with it, and exported as OSCAL.

```yaml
control_matrix:
  application:      app-wkb-dossier
  specification:    spec-4417@5
  spec_class:       generative                  # §6.4 — this field changes what is claimable
  consequence:      c4
  profile:          maestro-high                   # §5.5
  platform_grade:   evidenced                   # §7.1 — the ceiling on every inherited row
  frameworks:       [ iso-27001, nist-csf-2.0, soc2-tsc ]
  controls:
    - control:      iso-27001:A.8.15            # logging
      state:        inherited
      by:           PS2
      evidence:     ps2://export/tnt-x?from=…
      grade:        evidenced
    - control:      iso-27001:A.5.34            # privacy and PII protection
      state:        shared
      by:           PS7
      application_contribution:
        facet:      personal_data_in_scope
        declared:   true
        provenance: { source: declared, by: usr-p-visser }     # S4 — this is the weak link
      evidence:     ps7://classification-report/app-wkb-dossier
      grade:        evidenced
    - control:      iso-27001:A.6.1             # screening
      state:        client
      by:           null
      note:         "Outside the platform. Stated so the matrix is complete, never so it looks covered"
  unmapped:
    - control:      iso-27001:A.7.4             # physical monitoring
      reason:       not_applicable_to_software
```

**Three rules on this artifact.**

1. **`client` rows are printed, never omitted.** A matrix that shows only what the platform covers reads as a claim of completeness. §14.11's governance laundering is exactly this failure with a different subject.
2. **It is generated, so it cannot drift from the specification.** This belongs in §9's *generated per application* row alongside metric definitions and the dashboard (D38), and §13 records that as an upstream change.
3. **`unmapped` is a required section.** A framework control that maps to nothing must say so and why; silently dropping it is how a crosswalk becomes a fiction.

### 6.3 The inheritance ceiling — the rule that keeps this honest

> **An inherited control is never stronger than the platform's own claim grade for it, and an application is never certified because the platform is.**

Both halves matter and both will be pushed on.

- If the platform is *evidenced* for ISO 27001 and not certified, no application inherits *certified*. `platform_grade` sits at the top of the matrix for this reason and propagates down.
- **A certified platform does not certify an application.** ISO 27001 certifies our ISMS. An application running on it is *in scope of the client's own ISMS*, and what we supply is evidence for their audit, not a certificate for their system. Saying otherwise is §14.11's laundering with the platform as the beneficiary — the same shape as the conceptual design's §7.3 warranted-versus-as-is and D26's onboarding level on every record. It is **V-C**, and §6.5 is the partial answer.

### 6.4 Descriptive versus generative changes what is claimable

D27 and §14.5 already do this work and it lands here exactly:

| | **Generative specification** | **Descriptive specification** |
|---|---|---|
| Conformance | **Asserted** | **Assessed** |
| Inherited controls | The full never-generated row — the platform built it | **Only what has been substituted** (N3+), because the rest is code the platform did not write |
| Shared controls | From confirmed facets | From facets *reconstructed* from observation (S5) — visibly weaker |
| The matrix says | *This application implements* | *This application appears to implement* |

**An onboarded application at N1 inherits almost nothing**, and the matrix is the artifact that makes that visible instead of arguable. It is also the clearest possible sales argument for N3: **each substitution moves rows from `client` to `inherited`, and the conformance delta §14.7 already requires is exactly a diff of two matrices.** That gives the target architecture proposal a concrete, countable output it currently lacks.

### 6.5 What an application can never claim

Recorded because each will be asked for.

A certification (§6.3) · conformance for a party that is not its tenant (UC1 **C-B**) · substantive technical conformance where the standard requires professional judgement (UC1 §3.1) · a control in the `shared` state whose facet is unconfirmed · any control at a grade above `platform_grade` · anything at all, from a marketplace listing, beyond what re-running conformance in the consuming tenant establishes (conceptual §7.3).

### 6.6 The application conformance dossier

Collected, the per-application deliverables are a coherent product — and every one is generated:

**Control responsibility matrix** (§6.2) · **conformance record** (§5.8, PS12) · **SBOM and provenance** (PS13) · **architecture description** in 42010/C4 (§5.4) · **outcome and health dashboard** (§10.5, D38) · **data inventory** — categories, lawful bases, retention, subjects (PS7, and an art 30 record by construction) · **capability register** — granted versus attested (D32) · **exception ledger** with owners and expiries (§14.5) · **AI transparency statement** (V25) — which surfaces are model-facing, at which provider, model and version, under which egress class and processing jurisdiction, with the art 50 disclosure and marking in force.

**That set is the answer to "what can my application claim".** It is also, unusually, cheaper for the platform to produce than for the client to assemble by hand — which is the only durable form of a compliance product.

**One thing must be said about why the gap exists, because it will be asked in exactly these words.** PS15 §11.1 exempts the model provider from T21 on a stated and narrow ground: *the exit promise does not cover the one component that does not transfer.* That reasoning is sound, and it is about **exit**. Classes A and B are about **data**, and the exemption reaches neither. A rule waived for one reason is not waived for every reason — which is §2(a)'s defect exactly, *never overridable* read as *unconditional*, with the subject changed.

## 8. Regulation that binds the platform as a producer

Not optional, not negotiable by claim grade, and **three of the four have commencement dates inside the build plan.**

### 8.1 The four

| Instrument | Why in scope | Obligation | Machinery that exists |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NIS2** (Directive 2022/2555, NL: Cyberbeveiligingswet) | The platform is **ICT service management / a managed service provider** — Annex I. At N1 it holds standing production access (§14.10) | Registration; art 21 measures; **supply-chain security**; incident reporting on a 24h / 72h / 1-month clock; management liability | **PS14** holds the clocks, **PS8** delivers, **PS12** evidences. The clocks are not yet keyed to a regulatory deadline |
| **CRA** (Regulation 2024/2847) | Ambiguous and must be resolved. Pure SaaS is largely out; but **§13.5 hands open-code services and repositories to the client**, which may constitute placing a product on the market | Essential requirements; **SBOM**; vulnerability handling and coordinated disclosure; updates across a support period; reporting of exploited vulnerabilities | **T10's SBOM and PS13's ledger are the hard part and are committed.** §7 of the technical design defers the watch pipeline — CRA may remove the choice |
| **AI Act** (Regulation 2024/1689, **as amended by the Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744**) | The platform builds and operates AI systems and composes them into client applications | Role allocation (§8.2); **art 50 transparency, applicable since 2 August 2026**; art 4 AI literacy, applicable since February 2025; high-risk obligations **only if scope is reached**, and **deferred to 2 December 2027** for Annex III / 2 August 2028 for Annex I | **PS15 is an AI Act evidence engine already** — construct and model versions, refusals, oversight levels, ceilings. **Art 50 has nothing** (V7, `PLAT-AI-006`) |
| **GDPR** as processor **and** controller | Processor for tenant application data; controller for accounts, telemetry, its own records | Art 28 DPA and sub-processor disclosure; art 30 records; art 32; art 33 breach notification; art 35 DPIA support; art 44+ transfers | **PS7's classification envelope is an art 30 record by construction**; PS2 is the art 32 trail. **Art 28's sub-processor disclosure and art 44's transfer mechanism have no artifact at all** (V5, V21) — and EDPB Opinion 28/2024 sets a high bar for calling a model anonymous, so *the provider holds no personal data* is not available as an answer |

**Two dates moved and a supervisor arrived while this document was being written, and neither changes an obligation above.** The **Digital Omnibus** deferred the Annex III high-risk obligations to 2 December 2027 and the Annex I ones to 2 August 2028 — which touches only the heaviest set and **none of the three that already bind us**: art 4, art 50, and the GPAI chapter. And the Dutch **Uitvoeringswet AI-verordening**, consulted April to June 2026, designates **ten market surveillance authorities with the AP and the RDI coordinating**, with no national layer and no new regulator. What both change is **who asks**, which is what §7.2's `Evidenced by` column exists to answer. A build plan carrying a superseded date is worse than one carrying none, so §8.1 is dated rather than cited (**V26**).

**One more works in the platform's favour:** the **Data Act's** cloud-switching provisions require switching support, portability, and functional equivalence. **§13.5 and P9 already exceed it.** Available at *evidenced* grade for the cost of writing it down.

### 8.2 The platform's AI Act role, and where it is unresolved

| Party | Likely role | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| The platform, for its **own** seat agents | **Deployer** | Art 26 if any system is high-risk; art 4 literacy regardless |
| The platform, for a **generated application** containing AE5 | **Provider** — it develops the system and places it on the market under its own name | The heavier set. The tenant is then the deployer |
| The model vendor | GPAI provider | The platform is a downstream deployer, not a GPAI provider, while it does not modify the model |

That second row is undecided and determines how much of the Act lands here rather than on the client (**V-B**). It should close before AE5 is built.

### 8.3 P3 is a regulatory-scope control, and this was not why it was adopted

Annex III(4) covers employment and worker management, including task allocation and performance evaluation. **UC1's U4 (hour registration and CAO computation) and U5 (allocation) sit on that line**, and a platform that computed pay with a model would be operating a high-risk AI system.

It does not, because **P3 requires binding calculations to be deterministic versioned rules computed by AE3** — and the Act's recitals exclude systems that merely execute rules defined by natural persons. **The AE3/AE5 engine split is therefore a scope control as well as a defensibility control.** T14's boundary acquires a second, harder justification, and the argument holds only while AE3 genuinely computes: an AE5 that drafts and an AE3 that rubber-stamps is one system wearing two names.

### 8.4 Art 50 transparency, and why ISO 42001 is cheap here

**This section is no longer forward-looking, and v0.5's correction is that V7 is not a pending decision but an unmet obligation.** Art 50 has applied since **2 August 2026**. The only grace is until 2 December 2026, for the machine-readable marking of systems already on the market — which is not us, because nothing has shipped. `PLAT-AI-006` is where the obligation becomes checkable instead of noted.

**Art 50** requires people to be told when they are interacting with an AI system, and synthetic content to be marked machine-readably. Three surfaces are affected and none says so: UC1's **U6** portal conversational intake, **S1 conversational support** (§13.1, and W-C is open on where it lives), and any generated narrative — a drafted meerwerk description, a gap-register explanation — which is synthetic content shown to a human.

**ISO 42001, paired with NIST AI RMF's Govern/Map/Measure/Manage, is close to free.** Their core asks — an AI policy, risk and impact assessment, human oversight, lifecycle records over AI systems — are §11.1's autonomy ladder, §12's oversight ceilings, PS15's per-action refusal record with model and construct versions, and PS12's conformance record. **Evidenced grade is available roughly when PS15 build step 3 completes**, which no competitor produces as a by-product.

## 14. Open

- **V-A.** **Whether the platform is a CRA manufacturer.** Turns on whether §13.5's handover constitutes placing a product on the market. A question for counsel; until answered, build as if yes, since the expensive half is committed.
- **V-B.** **The platform's AI Act role for a generated application** — provider or component supplier. Should close before AE5.
- **V-C.** **Whether a certification can ever be claimed *for* an application**, or only for the platform. V14 is the position; the contractual wording is not drafted.
- **V-D.** **Where the Tier 1 catalogue and the crosswalks physically live** — the platform pack (UC1 **C-C** asks whether such a thing is a pack) or platform policy versioned with the release. Effective dating argues for the pack; changing with our own code argues the other way.
- **V-E.** **Whether ASVS level and accessibility target are set by consequence class alone, or a tenant may raise them.** §12.5 makes oversight a dial a client may tighten but never loosen; the same asymmetry probably applies, which makes it a tenant-binding field.
- **V-F.** **What the support period is**, for CRA and for §13.2's breaking-change notice. Two commitments, one number.
- **V-G.** **Whether the platform's own NIS2 incident clock is a binding calculation under P3.** Same shape as **T-S** and **W-B**, and the answer should be the same.
- **V-H.** **Whether the control matrix is versioned with the specification or produced continuously by PS12.** With the specification, it is a point-in-time claim that can go stale against a pack change; continuously, it is a live artifact whose history must still be reconstructable for an audit. Probably both, and the split is not obvious.
- **V-I.** **How a `shared` control degrades when its facet is `extracted` but unconfirmed** (S4). Refuse the claim, or carry it at a lower grade? Refusing is cleaner and may make the matrix unusably sparse early.

- **V-J.** **Whether a no-training, bounded-retention term is procurable at the capability tier the composition plane needs**, and what the fallback is if it is not — a self-hosted open-weights model at lower capability, or a narrower egress class that sends less. This is the commercial half of V20 and the only invariant in §3 the platform cannot satisfy by writing code.
- **V-K.** **Whether residency binds the model or only the data.** An EU-hosted endpoint operated by a non-EU-controlled provider satisfies class B's first ask and none of its fifth. The rijksbreed definition — *data localisation **and** operational autonomy* — says these are two requirements, and `PLAT-AI-005` currently expresses one.
- **V-L.** **Whether construct disclosure can be offered under audit without breaching E11 and D20.** Clients ask to see the prompts; PS15 exists so that prompts never transfer. A read-only attested inspection is the obvious compromise and it is not obviously compatible with either commitment.
- **V-M.** **Whether the platform is deployer or provider for art 50 on a generated application's surfaces.** **V-B's second instance**, and it binds sooner than V-B does, because art 50 is already in force while the high-risk set is not.
- **V-O.** **What a client's ISMS auditor makes of a shared runtime process.** `PLAT-RUN-001` permits a tenant's own applications to be co-resident, which is defensible and is not what an ISO 27001 auditor expects to hear about a *regulated* application. The consequence-class dial (T45) is the answer, and whether it should be the *default* above a class rather than available on request is not decided — and it is a cost decision dressed as a control decision, which is exactly the shape §12.5 warns about.
- **V-P.** **Whether `PLAT-RUN-006`'s idempotency key is claimable as an integrity control.** It prevents a duplicated regulated value, which reads as an integrity control to every framework in §7.2, and the platform enforces it at admission. Whether it can be claimed *inherited* for a generated application — the key is the platform's, the *effect* is the application's — is **§6.1's `shared` state** in its sharpest form, and S4's facet-quality problem applies unusually directly.
- **V-N.** **Whether embeddings, fine-tunes and vector stores are a PS7 shape, an export class, or out of scope.** P9 promises reconstruction from an export alone; a retrieval index derived from tenant data is either covered by that promise or is a silent exception to it.

*Inherited:* **T-S** and **W-B** (SLA clocks as binding calculations — V-G is their third instance), **E-H** (PS15 versus AE5 — §8.2's role allocation changes with the answer), **W-C** (where S1 support lives — art 50 lands on it either way), **C5** and **C-A** from UC1 (tenant-scoped configuration — V6 is the platform-side instance), **C-C** from UC1 (whether a platform pack is a pack — V-D is its third instance), and conceptual open question **O** (the user interface — claim grades must be visible in it).
